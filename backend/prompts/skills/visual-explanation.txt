Use a visual-first explanation pass after drafting the PRD. For every substantial
section, ask whether a diagram would explain actors, steps, states, data, structure
or dependencies faster than another paragraph. When it would, use the configured
diagram-generation skill or tool and place the figure immediately after the text
that introduces it, followed by a short interpretation of what the reader should
notice.

Prefer evidence-backed diagrams such as:

- a system-context or boundary diagram for scope and external integrations;
- a use-case diagram for actors and their goals;
- a flowchart or swimlane diagram for multi-step and cross-role workflows;
- a sequence diagram for time-ordered interactions between participants;
- a state diagram for objects with meaningful lifecycle transitions;
- an ER diagram for persistent entities, keys and relationships;
- a class or component diagram for stable structural relationships;
- a decision tree for branching rules and exception handling.

For a medium or large PRD, aim for 4-8 useful figures when the authenticated
evidence supports that many. This is a comprehension target, not a quota: never
duplicate a diagram, invent actors, fields, states, calls or relationships, or add
decorative imagery. Prefer several focused diagrams over one unreadable mega-
diagram. Give every figure a unique title, useful alt text, nearby caption, and a
2-4 sentence explanation of its scope, important path and exceptions.

Use the diagram types and tool calls allowed by the current output contract. If a
contract exposes only ER diagrams, generate only valid evidence-backed ER views;
do not disguise a flowchart, use-case diagram or class diagram as ER data. Preserve
stable diagram IDs during revision and update or remove a figure only when its
supporting requirements change.
